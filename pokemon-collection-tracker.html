The apps, briefly and honestly

Tracking + selling

PathBinder

Tracks Pokémon in English and Japanese alongside Magic, Yu-Gi-Oh! and One Piece. Its Pokémon strength is variant-accurate completion: each finish (Normal, Reverse Holo, Holo, 1st Edition) is its own slot, so a "200-card set" counts the ~350+ real collector slots and shows a master-set percentage — most checklists only track base numbers. It has a scanner, live prices, and a built-in marketplace where you sell your own cards at a 6–8% fee (about half of eBay/TCGplayer). Free tier is unlimited. Newer and smaller than the incumbents — the honest trade-off.

Set completion

Pokellector

The long-running, free Pokémon set-completion checklist. Clean and thorough for ticking off base set numbers across every English set. No scanner, no reverse-holo-as-separate-slot tracking, and no marketplace — it's a checklist, not a portfolio or store.

Portfolio analytics

Collectr

The strongest multi-TCG value tracker — stock-portfolio-style charts across raw, graded and sealed Pokémon in 25+ games, including Japanese sets. Its shop lets you buy, but there's no way to list your own cards to sell to other users, so selling happens off-platform.

Marketplace-first

TCGplayer

Primarily the largest US card marketplace, with a free tracker and scanner wired into it. Best if your priority is selling to the biggest buyer pool — the trade-off is seller fees around 13% and a tracker that's a feature of a store rather than the main event.

Scanning

Dragon Shield & CollX

Dragon Shield's apps are the fastest, most accurate scanners if bulk scanning is your priority (Pokémon-specific app; tracking only, no marketplace). CollX covers sports and TCG on one big database with a scanner and an in-app marketplace — a good low-friction start if you collect across categories, free up to 500 items.

How to choose

  • You want a true master set (reverse holos, variants counted) → PathBinder.
  • You collect Japanese cards too → PathBinder or Collectr.
  • You want to sell your Pokémon from the same app you track in → PathBinder (lowest fee), TCGplayer (biggest audience), or CollX.
  • You just want a free set checklist → Pokellector.
  • You scan huge bulk lots → Dragon Shield.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best app to track a Pokémon card collection?

It depends on what you want. For set-completion checklists, Pokellector. For value charts, Collectr. For a fast scanner, Dragon Shield. For tracking plus selling your own cards in one app — with master-set and reverse-holo completion and English + Japanese sets — PathBinder. All have free tiers.

Which Pokémon tracker counts reverse holos and master sets?

PathBinder tracks each finish as its own slot — Normal, Reverse Holo, Holo, 1st Edition — so completion includes reverse-holo variants and shows a master-set percentage. Pokellector focuses on base set-number completion.

Can I track Japanese Pokémon cards?

PathBinder and Collectr both include Japanese Pokémon sets alongside English. Pokellector and TCGplayer are primarily English-focused.

What is the cheapest way to sell Pokémon cards from a tracker?

Among apps that track your collection and let you sell in the same place, PathBinder's 6–8% seller fee is well below TCGplayer's ~13% and eBay's ~13–15% all-in. The trade-off is that eBay and TCGplayer have larger buyer audiences.

Is there a free Pokémon collection tracker?

Yes — Pokellector, Collectr, TCGplayer and PathBinder all have free tiers; PathBinder's is unlimited tracking with no card cap.
