Track and sell in one app

On TCGplayer, tracking your collection and running a store are separate experiences. In PathBinder they're the same one: scan a card into your binder, watch its value, and list it for sale without re-entering anything. When it sells, it leaves your collection automatically. For vendors and shops there's inventory splitting (on-shelf vs listed online) and an in-store point-of-sale, so your PathBinder binder stays the single source of truth across channels.

Where TCGplayer still wins — honestly

We're not going to pretend otherwise: TCGplayer has far more buyers than a newer marketplace does. If your only goal is to move volume as fast as possible and fees are an afterthought, that liquidity is real and it matters. Many sellers do both — list high-velocity singles where the buyers already are, and use PathBinder to track everything, sell at lower fees, and run their shop. PathBinder is built to be the low-fee home base, not to claim it out-traffics the incumbent on day one.

Frequently asked questions

Is PathBinder cheaper than TCGplayer to sell on?

Yes. PathBinder's seller fee is 6% to 8% depending on your plan, versus roughly 13% in combined fees on TCGplayer. On a $100 sale that's about $6-8 to PathBinder versus roughly $13 on TCGplayer.

Does PathBinder have as many buyers as TCGplayer?

No — TCGplayer is the largest card marketplace and has more buyers today. PathBinder is newer and growing. The trade-off is much lower seller fees and having your collection tracker and marketplace in one app.

Can I track my collection in PathBinder?

Yes, and it's the core of the app. PathBinder is a full collection tracker first with a marketplace built on top, so the cards you track are the cards you can list. The free plan includes unlimited tracking, the scanner, and live prices.

What games does PathBinder support?

Magic: The Gathering, Pokémon, Yu-Gi-Oh!, One Piece, and more — tracked and sold in one place.

How are buyers protected on PathBinder?

Payments run through Stripe with its standard dispute process. If there's a problem with an order, the buyer can open a dispute through the normal card-payment channels. PathBinder doesn't custodially hold funds.
